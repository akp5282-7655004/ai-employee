```text
SOP 147 · PAID ACQUISITION


Review a Google Ads Search campaign
before launch
Check the whole campaign against the business plan, assign any fixes and make a clear
ready-or-hold recommendation while ads stay off.



 ESTIMATED TIME           TOOLS                             RESPONSIBLE
 60 minutes               Google Ads, Google Sheets         The person checking whether a prepared
                                                            Search campaign is ready for the owner’s
                                                            launch decision.



Before you start
Requirements
  ●​ Access to inspect the intended Google Ads campaign, its settings, keywords, ads and assets.
  ●​ A saved unpublished Search draft or an existing paused Search campaign to review.
  ●​ A business owner who can confirm the offer, intended customers, desired action, spending limit
        and launch authority.
  ●​ The campaign plan and keyword research used to build this campaign, plus prepared ad copy and
        any tracking test evidence.
  ●​ Access to open the campaign’s intended landing pages and review their customer action.


 THIS SOP COVERS                                       THIS DOES NOT COVER
       ●​ One prepared Search campaign across            ●​ Activating or publishing the campaign,
          all intended ad groups, ads, keywords              spending or changing billing.
          and assets.                                    ●​ Installing tracking, rewriting ads or
       ●​ Checking goals and measurement,                   completing campaign repairs during the
          spending, reach, offer alignment,                 review.
          destinations and outstanding warnings.         ●​ Historical performance audits and client
       ●​ A Ready or Hold recommendation with               onboarding.
          specific fixes, owners and rechecks.

Related SOPs
  ●​    SOP 145: Research and organize Google Ads keywords with Semrush
  ●​    SOP 149: Add sitelinks, callouts and structured snippets to Google Search ads
  ●​    SOP 130: Write and save a responsive Search ad in Google Ads
  ●​    SOP 039: Choose the right Google Ads conversion tracking method
  ●​    SOP 129: Review Search terms and add negative keywords in Google Ads
1. Set the scope and a fair decision rule
1.1 Open the Search Campaign Planning Workbook used to prepare the campaign. If it was
built without the workbook, make a copy and fill the campaign setup fields in Brief from the
owner’s decisions and Keywords from the original research; record missing decisions as Not
provided. Read Guide and the fictional Example. Use Review for one row per issue or
completed check.

1.2 In Google Ads, select the customer account and campaign or reopen the saved draft.
Match the name and offer to Brief → Campaign name. In Review, add a Campaign
identity/state row with the account identifier, campaign name and observed Draft or Paused
state under Observed. If it is enabled, stop this review and tell the launch owner; do not assume
a future date makes it off.

1.3 For each Review row, fill Area, Expected, Observed, Evidence/link and Result. Use
Pass when the account agrees with a supported business decision; Blocker for something
wrong that could waste spend or break the customer journey; Unknown when proof or an
owner decision is missing; Improvement for a useful enhancement that does not stop the
agreed plan. Ready requires every required area checked with no unresolved Blocker or
Unknown.


2. Check the goal, money and audience together
2.1 Compare the intended customer action with the campaign’s selected conversion goals, the
actions inside them and the tracking owner’s dated test proof. A conversion action is a
particular event Google counts, such as a completed order; a goal groups related actions.
     Then follow: SOP 039: Choose the right Google Ads conversion tracking method

     a. For a saved new-campaign draft, open Review → Overview → Goal and reopen the
     conversion-goal selection if the wizard offers it. For an existing paused campaign, open Campaign
     settings → Conversion goals. Compare the saved goals and action names with Brief → Primary
     conversion. In Review, add a Goals/measurement row naming the selection. If the draft leaves
     Goal blank or does not expose the selection, record exactly that as Unknown, assign the tracking
     owner to inspect it before launch and keep Hold. Do not infer saved goals from account defaults or
     an earlier selection. An unrelated selected goal or action is a Blocker.

     b. Open Goals → Conversions → Summary and expand the relevant goal to inspect its actions.
    Read each action’s Action optimization setting; open its details if needed. Primary normally
    means an action can guide bidding when its standard goal is selected. Secondary normally means
    it is reported for observation instead. In Review’s Observed field, name the action and setting. If you
    cannot inspect it, record Unknown and ask the tracking owner for that evidence.

    c. Check whether the campaign uses a custom goal, a manually chosen collection of actions. If so,
    open that goal under Custom goals in the conversions summary and inspect every included action.
    A selected custom goal can use a secondary action for bidding too. Compare the whole list with the
    intended customer action; an unrelated included event is a Blocker. If the campaign uses only
    standard goals, record that finding instead.

    d. Read the tracking owner’s dated proof for a successful intended action, an unsuccessful action
    that is not counted, and duplicate handling. Put the proof in Review’s Evidence/link field. Missing
    proof is Unknown; known unrelated or duplicate counting is a Blocker. A green status or completed
    setup page alone does not prove the correct action is counted.

2.2 Compare the saved budget, bidding strategy and any configured limits or targets with the
owner’s plan. Record Budget/bidding findings in Review.

    a. Read the saved budget currency and amount, then compare them with Brief’s owner-approved
    monthly and average daily figures. A different saved amount is a Blocker; a missing owner amount
    or spending approval is Unknown.

    b. Check the saved strategy against Brief’s Bidding/reason and measurement proof. Conversion
    bidding needs verified intended measurement before launch. Maximize conversions without a
    target CPA is a valid choice when measurement is verified and the budget is approved; the missing
    optional target is not an issue by itself. Click bidding needs an explicitly agreed traffic-test purpose.

    c. If a limit or target is actually configured or required by the agreed strategy, inspect its amount and
    the owner’s supporting reason. A configured target CPA without a defensible basis, or a required
    click limit not supplied, is Unknown until the owner resolves it. Do not invent a target just to fill the
    workbook.

2.3 Inspect the campaign settings and add separate Review rows for Network,
Locations/presence, Language and Schedule/time zone. Compare with Brief. For this
manual first-campaign method, Google Search only, the intended locations, and Presence
should be selected. Check excluded areas as well as included areas. Read ad and page
language together; use a language selector if your account still shows one during the Search
rollout. Compare schedule hours in the account’s time zone with fulfilment needs.
3. Follow each intent from search to action
3.1 Work through every included ad group in Keywords. Compare its entered keywords and
match types with the research decision, then inspect saved negative keywords and any
applicable shared or inherited lists. For an unpublished draft, also inspect the planned negatives
and identify any not yet applied. In Review add Keywords/exclusions rows for mismatches
and conflicts. Check that useful included phrases are not blocked.

3.2 For every included group, compare the saved or draft responsive ad with its intended
search, offer, required wording and destination. Check AI Max, Text customization and Final
URL expansion against the manual plan. Inspect relevant sitelinks, callouts and snippets,
including inherited account associations, for unrelated offers, expired claims and wrong links.
Add Ads/assets findings in Review. Use the asset guide for repairs.
     Then follow: SOP 149: Add sitelinks, callouts and structured snippets to Google Search ads

3.3 Open each distinct final URL and asset URL directly in a browser; do not click your own live
ad. Check on desktop and a mobile-size view that the page loads, matches the offer, shows
important conditions and makes the intended action clear. Ask the owner or tracking owner for a
safe form or checkout test when needed. Record URLs/customer action findings and dated
proof in Review. An untested action is Unknown; a broken action is a Blocker.

3.4 Read the wizard review page or the paused campaign’s diagnostics and ad/asset status. In
Review add Warnings/completeness with each material warning and what it affects. Check
every included group has its planned keywords and ad. A billing or advertiser-verification
warning may require the account owner before launch; assign it without changing billing or
accepting terms here. Draft ads may not have completed policy review, so say Policy review
pending rather than claiming approval.


4. Turn the findings into an owner decision
4.1 For every Blocker or Unknown in Review, enter Fix, Owner and Due date. Describe the
exact change or missing proof, not “optimize campaign.” Example: Tracking owner: show one
successful repair request counted once, plus a failed form attempt not counted, by
Friday. Group repeated findings only when one fix resolves all affected groups.

4.2 After the assigned person completes a fix, repeat the affected check and fill Recheck
evidence; change Result only when that proof supports it. In Review → Ready/Hold, enter
Ready if every required area passes and no Blocker or Unknown remains; otherwise enter
Hold. Fill Decision reason, Campaign state, Approver and Approval date; leave approval
date blank until the actual owner decides. Give the workbook to that owner for a separate
launch decision.
```

```text
SOP 149 · PAID ACQUISITION


Add sitelinks, callouts and structured
snippets to Google Search ads
Save relevant Search ad assets with useful destinations, supported claims and the correct
campaign or ad-group associations.



 ESTIMATED TIME         TOOLS                             RESPONSIBLE
 50 minutes             Google Ads, Google Sheets         The person researching or preparing a
                                                          Search campaign for a business.



Before you start
Requirements
  ●​ Google Ads access that can create assets and associate them with the intended Search campaign
      or ad group.
  ●​ An existing Search campaign, its ad groups and ad copy, with the offer, target language and
     landing pages agreed by the business owner.
  ●​ Public website pages and owner-confirmed product facts, benefits and service terms you can use
     in assets.


 THIS SOP COVERS                                     THIS DOES NOT COVER
    ●​ Plan and save manual sitelinks, callouts         ●​ Campaign launch, serving or
       and structured snippets.                             performance measurement.
    ●​ Choose campaign or ad-group scope and            ●​ Account-wide asset changes,
       inspect higher-level assets.                        automated-asset settings and other asset
    ●​ Read back text, destinations,                       types.
       associations and review status.                  ●​ Writing the responsive search ad itself or
                                                           changing shared assets used elsewhere.

Related SOPs
  ●​ SOP 145: Research and organize Google Ads keywords with Semrush
  ●​ SOP 130: Write and save a responsive Search ad in Google Ads
  ●​ SOP 039: Choose the right Google Ads conversion tracking method


1. Plan assets for the intended offer
1.1 Make a copy of the Search Campaign Planning Workbook, or open your existing campaign
copy. Use Assets for this procedure. Look at the fictional asset examples in Example before
filling your own rows.

1.2 Open Google Ads, choose the intended account, then open the Search campaign.
Compare its campaign and ad-group names with the owner’s agreed scope.
     ⚠ Warning: Adding assets to an enabled campaign can make them eligible to appear after review.
     Use a paused campaign when preparing for launch; changing campaign status belongs to the
     launch owner.

1.3 Give each planned asset one clear job.

     a. Use Sitelink for a useful extra page, such as a size guide, delivery information or a relevant
     product range. In Assets, create a row per link and fill Asset type, Text, Description 1, Description 2,
     and Final URL. Use distinct link text and relevant destinations on the ad’s website. Open every URL
     and check that its content matches the label.

     b. Use Callout for a short, supported benefit without a link. In a new row, fill Asset type and Text.
     For example, “Assembly Guide Included” works only when the business includes one. Check the ad
     and other assets so the callout adds information rather than repeating the same phrase.

     c. Use Structured snippet for a group of products or services under a preset header. In Language /
     header, enter the ad language and a matching header; in Values, list individual items. For example,
     Types: Standing desks; Desk converters; Desk risers; Desktop shelves. Each must really be offered.
     Use at least three distinct values, preferably four useful ones. Keep promotional benefits in callouts.

1.4 Choose the level where each asset belongs.
     ⚠ Warning: Do not assume a campaign sitelink hides an unrelated account sitelink. Ask the
     account owner to resolve unrelated higher-level assets before launch. Editing an existing shared
     asset changes it wherever it is associated.

     a. In each row’s Level and Campaign / ad group, enter Campaign when the asset suits all ad
     groups in that campaign; use Ad group for an offer or benefit unique to one group. Put the page or
     owner-approved fact supporting each claim in Claim source / relevance.

     b. Open Assets in the Campaigns menu and use the Associations table view. Inspect sitelinks,
     callouts and snippets for the selected campaign or group, including higher-level associations. In
     Existing asset / inherited check, note what already fits and what conflicts. Sitelinks from higher levels
     can appear alongside lower-level ones; ad-group callouts and snippets replace their higher-level
     equivalents.

1.5 Review your planned set against the campaign’s offers and customer questions. Add
enough distinct useful pages and benefits to cover them. Aim for four useful sitelinks and
several distinct callouts; add more when they serve a real purpose. If the site cannot support
useful extra links or a truthful snippet list, put the missing page or facts and owner in Next action
/ owner.


2. Create the planned assets
2.1 Save the ready sitelinks from your plan.

     a. In Assets, select the plus button, then Sitelink. In Add to, choose Campaign or Ad group and
     select the target from your row. Choose Create new for new text. If an unchanged existing sitelink
     already matches your row exactly, select Use existing and select that sitelink; keep its shared
     content unchanged.

     b. For Create new, enter the planned Sitelink text, both Description lines and Final URL. In English,
     keep link text within 25 characters and each description line within 35; follow the live counters for
     your language. Read the preview and fix field errors. For Use existing, check the selected saved text
     and destination against your plan. Select Save to save the new asset or add the unchanged existing
     asset’s association. Repeat for every ready sitelink row.

2.2 In Assets, select plus → Callout. Choose the planned level and target under Add to. Select
Create new, enter the ready Callout text and check the character counter; English callouts allow
25 characters. Select Save and repeat for the remaining callouts. Use existing only when the
unchanged text and meaning already fit.

2.3 In Assets, select plus → Structured snippet. Choose the planned Add to target, language
and header. Enter each planned item in its own value field, using Add value as needed. English
values allow 25 characters each. Check that all items fit the header, then select Save. Repeat
for each ready header set.
     ⚠ Warning: Put one item in each value field. A comma-separated list in one field, repeated items or
     promotional phrases such as “Free shipping” can be disapproved.



3. Check the saved associations and hand off
3.1 Read back each asset type after saving.

     a. Return to Assets → Associations, select each asset type and find your saved rows. Confirm
     Level and campaign or ad group against the plan. Open each asset’s details or edit view to compare
     text, sitelink descriptions and URLs, or snippet language, header and individual values. Close
     without changing it after the check.
      b. In Saved association / status, enter the observed review status for each row after confirming its
      saved level and target. Under review can be expected after saving. For a Disapproved or limited
      status, open the status details and put the stated reason and responsible person in Next action /
      owner. For your newly created asset, correct the named problem and repeat the saved check; do
      not edit a shared asset without checking its other uses.

3.2 Open every saved sitelink destination directly and check the final page still matches the
saved label and offer. Compare the finished Assets rows with the campaign’s offers and the
inherited check. Put any missing asset, conflicting message, broken destination or unfinished
review issue and owner in Next action / owner.
      ⚠ Warning: A saved or eligible asset is not guaranteed to appear. Google chooses assets for each
      auction; serving is outside this setup check.

3.3 Give the completed Assets tab and any open issues to the person responsible for reviewing
the campaign. Ask them to confirm the saved assets are ready as part of their full campaign
review and resolve open issues before the launch owner enables advertising.
```

<details><summary>Full SOP text</summary>

```text
SOP 143 · PAID ACQUISITION


Build and review a first Standard Shopping
campaign before launch
Review a first Shopping campaign’s products and spending settings, then give the owner a
complete plan to approve before launch.



 ESTIMATED TIME           TOOLS                           RESPONSIBLE
 45 minutes               Google Ads, Google Merchant     The person preparing an online store’s first
                          Center                          Shopping campaign for the business
                                                          owner to review.



Before you start
Requirements
  ●​ Standard or Administrator access to the business’s Google Ads advertiser account; its name,
        customer ID, and account currency.
  ●​ Access to the linked Merchant Center account and genuine online products approved for Shopping
     ads in the intended country; working checkout, shipping, and returns.
  ●​ An available business owner who can decide the products, delivery country, monthly advertising
     allowance, maximum click cost, and who will approve launch.
  ●​ The name of the store’s tested Google Ads purchase conversion action, with its transaction value
     and currency checked by the person responsible for tracking.


 THIS SOP COVERS                                     THIS DOES NOT COVER
       ●​ Prepare one unpublished Standard              ●​ Publish or enable campaigns, add billing,
          Shopping setup for one online store and          or spend money
          one target country                            ●​ Performance Max, local inventory,
       ●​ Select exact product Item IDs, purchase          vehicles, or multiple overlapping
          goals, an average daily budget, and              Shopping campaigns
          Maximize Clicks bidding with a bid limit      ●​ Install purchase tracking, repair product
       ●​ Review the setup and preserve its                disapprovals, or run ongoing bidding
          complete settings in a private note for          optimization
          owner review before launch

Related SOPs
  ●​    SOP 138: Set up Merchant Center and verify the store website
  ●​    SOP 139: Configure Merchant Center shipping and returns
  ●​    SOP 140: Connect WooCommerce products to Merchant Center
  ●​    SOP 141: Diagnose and fix common Merchant Center product disapprovals
  ●​    SOP 142: Link Merchant Center to an owned Google Ads account
  ●​ SOP 096: Create a Google Ads account without launching a campaign
  ●​ SOP 016: Grant an agency user access to a Google Ads account
  ●​ SOP 021: Track WooCommerce purchases in Google Ads with Google Tag Manager


1. Make the first campaign’s plan
Shopping ads use your product data to create ads. Standard Shopping lets you control which
products and bids belong in this campaign.

1.1 Create a private Shopping campaign note with four sections: Accounts (Ads name,
customer ID, currency, Merchant Center ID); Plan (campaign name, included products and Item
IDs, excluded products and Item IDs, feed label or country of sale, target country, purchase
action, monthly allowance, daily budget, bid limit, launch reviewer); Review (checked settings,
Item ID selection, excluded products, EU political-ads answer, AI Max setting, and warnings);
Handoff (form status, checked date, catalog fix, next action). Use this note throughout the
guide.

1.2 Ask the owner which products they want to promote first and where orders can be
delivered. In Merchant Center, open Products → All products and inspect those products’
Shopping ads approval, availability, online channel, and delivery countries. Choose products the
owner can keep in stock and fulfil in one country. Write their names and the country under Plan
→ Included products. Illustrative example: a store sells mugs and posters but can reliably ship
mugs to Portugal; choose its available mugs and Portugal.
    ⚠ Warning: If the intended products are disapproved or lack delivery coverage, have the store
    owner resolve that prerequisite before building the campaign.

1.3 Identify the real product source and copy the exact Item IDs that will include your chosen
products.
    ⚠ Warning: Use Merchant Center’s product ID, not a product name, Google category number, or a
    parent item-group ID. A WooCommerce product ID or SKU is usable only when it exactly matches
    the ID shown in Merchant Center.

    a. Open Settings → Data sources in Merchant Center and inspect the source containing the
    chosen products. Write its existing feed label, or country of sale when that is the available selection,
    in Plan. A label selects product sources, not the country where ads show. Inspect every source
    sharing that label so another source cannot add unexpected products.

    b. Return to Products → All products and inspect each intended product in the source. Copy its
    ID [id] into Plan → Included products and Item IDs, preserving its spelling and case. Item IDs
     identify individual offers. Each size or color variant has its own ID; include every variant the owner
     wants to advertise rather than its shared Item group ID [item_group_id].

     c. Compare the full source inventory with the owner’s intended product list. Under Plan →
     Excluded products and Item IDs, record the products and variants that must stay out. Example
     only: include the two mug IDs mug-blue and mug-red, and exclude poster-art and plate-white.
     Only the listed mug IDs will be selected; matching names or categories cannot add other items.
     Check every included offer is online and approved for Shopping ads in the target country.

     d. If an intended offer is missing, has an unclear ID, or is not approved for the target country, list its
     name and the missing result under Handoff → Catalog fix. Ask the catalog person to correct the
     source or approval issue and return its actual processed Merchant Center Item ID and country
     eligibility. Repeat the preceding checks after that readback before continuing.

1.4 Agree the spending amounts with the owner and enter them in Plan.
     ⚠ Warning: An average daily budget is not a hard daily cap. For most campaigns, Google can bill
     up to twice it in one day and 30.4 times it in a month when the budget stays unchanged. The owner
     must accept that spending pattern.

     a. Write the monthly allowance and account currency. Divide the allowance by 30.4 to choose the
     average daily budget; round down if rounding up would exceed the allowance. Illustrative example:
     an allowance of EUR 304 gives EUR 10 per day. This is a spending plan, not a prediction of sales.

     b. Ask the owner for the highest cost they are willing to pay for one visit during the first traffic test.
     Write it as Bid limit. Maximize Clicks lets Google choose bids to get visits within the budget; the
     limit constrains those bids. Example: the owner chooses EUR 0.40 for this test. That number is
     illustrative, not a recommended price. If no limit is agreed, prepare the other plan fields and wait
     before entering a bid amount.

1.5 In Google Ads, choose the advertiser and open Goals → Conversions → Summary. Find
the tested purchase action from your prerequisites and inspect its settings. Record its name in
Plan and confirm it counts completed orders, carries the order value and currency, and is a
primary action for the Purchase goal. A cart visit is not a purchase. If tracking still needs setup,
have the tracking person follow the linked SOP before completing this preparation.
     ⚠ Warning: Maximize Clicks seeks visits, not profitable sales. Purchase tracking is still needed to
     judge those visits before the owner launches the campaign.
     Then follow: SOP 021: Track WooCommerce purchases in Google Ads with Google Tag Manager



2. Choose the campaign and product scope
Use the new-campaign flow and keep it unpublished. Standard Shopping does not save a
resumable browser draft; your note will preserve the settings for owner review. Publish
campaign starts a separate launch task.

2.1 Confirm the advertiser’s name and customer ID, and record them with its currency and
Merchant Center ID under Accounts. Open Campaigns → Campaigns, select the plus button,
then New campaign. Choose Sales as the objective.

2.2 Review the conversion goals offered for this campaign. Use the Purchase goal containing
the action in your note; remove unrelated lead, phone-call, or page-view goals from this
campaign’s selection. If a choice between account-default and campaign-specific goals
appears, use campaign-specific goals when the defaults include those unrelated results. Write
the selected goal and purchase action under Review.
     ⚠ Warning: Do not change the account’s default goals or the shared conversion action to fix this
     campaign. Have the tracking person resolve an incorrect action before continuing.

2.3 Choose Shopping, then the linked Merchant Center account matching Accounts. Select
Standard Shopping when the subtype choice appears, rather than accepting a Performance
Max recommendation. Enter a campaign name that includes the country and product group,
such as Shopping | Portugal | Mugs. Write that name in Plan.
     ⚠ Warning: The Merchant Center account cannot be changed after a campaign is created. If the
     correct account is absent, complete the account link before continuing.

2.4 Choose the feed label or country of sale from Plan. Compare the selected source with the
real source recorded in step 1.3. This selection may contain both included and excluded
products; the Item ID selection in step 3.4 will narrow it. Write the selected source under Review
→ Product scope.


3. Set spending and delivery settings
Use the agreed traffic-test settings. Stronger sales bidding needs dependable purchase data
and a separate decision about acceptable returns.

3.1 On Budget and bidding optimization, enter the average daily budget from Plan. Choose
Maximize Clicks and set its maximum CPC bid limit to the owner’s limit. CPC means cost per
click. Compare both amounts and the displayed currency with the note, then write them under
Review. If Google recommends a larger budget or another strategy, compare it with the owner’s
allowance before accepting any change.
    ⚠ Warning: If this flow does not offer Maximize Clicks with a bid limit, record the missing option and
    pause. Do not substitute an unexplained return-on-ad-spend target to continue.

3.2 In Campaign settings → Locations, replace any broad country selection with the target
country in Plan. Open Location options, when offered, and choose Presence: People in or
regularly in your included locations for this delivery-limited first campaign. Under Networks,
keep Google Search and turn off Google search partners for the first review. Write the checked
selections under Review.
    ⚠ Warning: Location detection is approximate. A feed label named PT does not itself restrict ads to
    Portugal; the campaign’s location setting does that.

3.3 In Campaign settings → EU political ads, answer whether this campaign has European
Union political ads. For an ordinary commercial product campaign with no political advertising,
select No, this campaign doesn’t have EU political ads. If its purpose is political or you are
unsure, ask the owner to review Google’s political-content definition before answering. Record
the answer under Review → EU political-ads answer.
    ⚠ Warning: Leave the separate checkbox declaring the account will not run EU political ads
    unchecked. It applies the declaration to other campaigns, beyond this campaign’s preparation.

3.4 Enter one ad group name describing the chosen products, such as Mugs, then set its
product scope.
    ⚠ Warning: If the unpublished setup lacks the required criteria or cannot show the selected
    inventory, record the missing control under Review and pause. Publishing first and filtering later
    would launch a broader campaign.

    a. Open the first ad group’s Product groups selection. Choose Use a selection of products, then
    set Select products by: to Item ID. Select every included ID from Plan. To enter the list together,
    choose Bulk add values manually and enter only the actual Merchant Center IDs, one per line.
    Check the selected list, then select Save.

    b. Reopen Product groups and compare every selected Item ID with Plan → Included products
    and Item IDs. Confirm all intended IDs are present, no extra ID is selected, and none from
    Excluded products and Item IDs appears. Keep Use a selection of products selected. Write the
    saved IDs and the included/excluded result under Review. If IDs or products do not match, correct
    the selection before continuing.
         ⚠ Warning: Changing Select products by: to another attribute clears the selection. Use the
         Item ID list throughout this method.
4. Review the setup and preserve the owner’s plan
Save the checked settings in your private note. The owner reviews that plan before a separate
launch decision.

4.1 Review the unpublished setup against Shopping campaign note before handing the plan
to the owner.
      ⚠ Warning: Stop before Publish campaign. A future start date or an intention to pause later does
      not replace leaving this preparation unpublished.

      a. After the ad group, open AI Max when offered. Keep Optimize with AI Max off for this initial
      review; it adds automated title and landing-page changes beyond the plan. Record AI Max: Off
      under Review, then select Next to open Summary or Review.

      b. Compare the campaign type, merchant, source, exact Item ID selection, included and excluded
      products, purchase goal, target country, networks, EU political-ads answer, daily budget, bidding
      strategy, bid limit, and ad group with the note. Select each section to inspect its settings when the
      summary omits a value. Correct mismatches using the setup navigation and write any unresolved
      warning, responsible person, and next action under Review.

4.2 Save the Shopping campaign note with every checked value from step 4.1, the complete
included/excluded Item ID lists, unresolved warnings and their owners. Add today’s date and the
form’s current status under Handoff. Keep the form open if the reviewer will inspect it in this
browser. If you need to close it, record Setup must be re-entered from this note under
Handoff → Form status; the campaign preparer will re-enter and recheck those settings after
review.
```

```text
     a. Write two options that clearly name the customer’s need, then add different useful facts, the
     business name and an action. Each headline must make sense on its own. For a fictional bike
     repair shop, assuming these facts are confirmed: Bike Puncture Repair, Bicycle Tyre Repairs,
     Oak Street Cycle Workshop, Local Bike Repair Workshop, Repair Your Bike Tyre, Find Us on
     Oak Street, Book a Repair Visit, Ask About Repair Options.

     b. Compare your options before keeping them. Bike Tyre Repairs / Puncture Repairs / Bicycle
     Repair Service mostly repeat one idea. Replace a repeated idea with a different supported fact.
     Guaranteed 10-Minute Repair adds a promise: remove it unless the owner confirms it is true for
     every customer who sees the ad. Keep fewer useful headlines rather than inventing facts to reach a
     count.

2.2 In Ad plan, add Descriptions. Write 2–4 descriptions, each up to 90 characters including
spaces, that explain the offer and next action.

     a. Give each description a complete message. For the fictional shop: Need a puncture repair?
     Ask Oak Street Cycle Workshop about repair options. and Visit our bike repair page to find
     our workshop and request a repair visit. Use your own confirmed details; these are examples,
     not facts about your business.

     b. Read each headline with each description. Then read groups of three headlines in different
     orders. Remove phrases that depend on another line: Only This Week plus Free Delivery could
     imply the wrong deadline if shown separately. Put any necessary condition beside the claim itself, or
     remove the claim when it cannot fit truthfully. In Ad plan, correct the affected text.

2.3 Choose how to handle Required wording in Ad plan. Google can rearrange the text
pieces, called assets. Pinning fixes one piece to a chosen position.

     a. If exact text must appear every time, include it in one complete description and mark that line Pin
    to Description position 1 in Ad plan. Fictional example: an owner requires Repairs by
    appointment only. Put that wording in the first description. Do not put it only in a later description
    that may be omitted.

    b. If Required wording is None, write No pins in Ad plan. Pinning limits the combinations Google
    can choose, so use it for a genuine fixed-wording requirement rather than to force a favourite
    preview.



3. Create, preview and save the ad
3.1 Return to the selected ad group. Open Campaigns > Ads, select the + button and choose
Responsive search ad. Select the intended campaign and ad group if Google asks.

3.2 Enter the text from Ad plan into the corresponding fields.

    a. Paste Final URL. The optional Display path is the short text shown after the website address; it
    does not change where the click goes. If you use it, enter a simple description such as bike-repair,
    up to 15 characters per path field. Add Display path to Ad plan and record your text, or Blank.

    b. If the editor contains suggested headlines or descriptions, use Clear prefills if offered, or clear
    that suggested text. Enter only the planned headlines and descriptions, each in its own field. Use the
    field’s counter to shorten overlong text without removing an important condition. Google requires at
    least three headlines and two descriptions to save. Update Ad plan with any changes.

    c. For the description marked Pin to Description position 1, select its pin icon and choose Show
    only in position 1 in the description’s menu. Pin no alternative description to that position unless it
    contains the same compulsory wording. If the plan says No pins, check the entered text is
    unpinned.
         ⚠ Warning: If Google warns that the pin applies only to the original URL, stop before saving
         and ask the owner to check the campaign’s copy/page settings.

3.3 Review the preview and Google’s feedback before saving.

    a. Use the available preview controls to inspect different combinations and device layouts. Check
    that the offer, business name, required wording and visitor action are understandable. The preview
    shows examples, not every possible display. Recheck the text-pairing exercise from step 2.2 for
    combinations the preview omits.

    b. Fix required-field errors. Read Ad strength, Google’s feedback on the variety and relevance of
    your text. Improve repeated or vague copy using confirmed facts. The rating is not a sales forecast;
    keep compulsory wording and truthful claims even if the rating stays lower. Update Ad plan with any
      correction.

3.4 Select Save and find the new ad in Ads for the intended ad group.

3.5 Reopen the saved ad using its edit control and compare its fields with Ad plan.

      a. Read the saved Final URL, display paths, all headlines, all descriptions and pin positions.
      Confirm each matches Ad plan. Open the saved Final URL in another tab and confirm it still shows
      the offer and visitor action. If anything differs, correct that new ad, save and repeat this check.

      b. Return to Campaigns and confirm the campaign still says Paused. Reopen the campaign’s AI
      Max settings and confirm AI Max, Text customization and Final URL expansion are still off. In Ad
      plan, add Review handoff with the saved ad’s headline, campaign, ad group and visible status.
      Give this note to the person who reviews the ad before launch, or keep it as your own review record.
      Saving is complete; launch is a separate decision.
```

```text
This can change which campaigns use Purchase by default.
         ⚠ Warning: A goal-level account-default change can affect which campaigns use Purchase by

     j. If an existing Purchase goal differs from the owner’s choice, leave it unchanged. Record the

     a. On the setup summary, select Use Google Tag Manager. If Google shows Set up in Google
     Tag Manager, open that setup panel so you can read the identifiers, then continue with the manual
     Tag Manager steps in this guide.

     b. Copy the numeric Conversion ID and Conversion label into WooCommerce purchase
     tracking note under Google Ads. Keep the pair together. When Tag Manager asks for a Google
     tag ID, use AW- followed by the number. When the conversion tag asks for a Conversion ID, use the
     number without AW-.
         ⚠ Warning: A label from another conversion action can send conversions to the wrong action
         in Google Ads.

     c. If Google Ads shows Enhanced conversions or User-provided data, record the visible state in
     WooCommerce purchase tracking note. Continue only when it is Off, Not configured, or Not
     shown. If it is On or otherwise enabled, stop and hand the new action to the Ads owner or
     maintainer; do not disable it. This guide does not send customer details.

     d. Select Finish or the button that closes the setup summary. Keep WooCommerce purchase
     tracking note open for the WordPress and Tag Manager steps.

dynamic ecommerce fields and sends them to the purchase conversion action.

3.1 Check for existing Google tag coverage before adding anything, then reuse one matching

     ⚠ Warning: Duplicate site-wide Google tags with the same ID can cause measurement problems.

     a. Open Google Tag Manager, select the installed Web container, and open Tags. Find Google tags
     whose Tag ID is AW- followed by the Conversion ID in WooCommerce purchase tracking note.
     Write the matching tag's name and trigger under Google Tag Manager in the note.
     b. If one matching enabled Google tag uses Initialization - All Pages, use it. If it uses a different
     trigger, or more than one matching tag is enabled, ask the container maintainer to leave one
     matching tag using Initialization - All Pages before continuing. This avoids adding a second tag for
     the same Google Ads account.

     c. If no matching Google tag is enabled, select New, choose Google tag, enter AW- plus the
     numeric Conversion ID, set Triggering to Initialization - All Pages, and save it with a clear name
     such as Google tag — Google Ads. Initialization - All Pages means the base Google tag loads as
     each page starts, so it can cover the whole website.

     d. Open the chosen Google tag and write its name, ID, and trigger in WooCommerce purchase
     tracking note under Google Tag Manager.

3.2 Check the container's click-data coverage and keep one valid path for connecting an ad
click to a later conversion.

     a. In Tags, find any enabled Conversion Linker tags. A Google tag loads the Ads measurement
     base; a Conversion Linker stores ad-click information from the landing-page URL so Google Ads
     can connect that click to a later purchase. Google's current guidance says a separate linker is
     unnecessary when a Google tag loads on every page, so check the existing coverage first.

     b. If an enabled Conversion Linker already fires on All Pages, use it and write Existing linker
     reused in WooCommerce purchase tracking note.

     c. If no all-page linker exists, open Workspace > Overview in GTM and find the Container quality
     card. If the card lists issues, select View [number] issues. If it says Good, select View
     recommendations. If the list says a Conversion Linker is missing, create one in the next substep. If
     it does not, and the Google tag uses Initialization - All Pages, write Google tag covers all pages;
     no separate linker needed in WooCommerce purchase tracking note.

     d. If the GTM diagnostic says a linker is missing, select New, choose Conversion Linker, use All
     Pages, and save one clear name such as Conversion Linker — All Pages. Write Linker added in
     WooCommerce purchase tracking note.

3.3 Open Variables and check for existing Data Layer Variables before creating anything.
Reuse a variable only when its data-layer path and Data Layer Version Version 2 match
exactly; otherwise create one. Record each chosen variable name and path under Google Tag
Manager in WooCommerce purchase tracking note.

     a. Choose or create the value variable. For an existing variable, inspect Data Layer Variable
     Name; if creating one, select New > Data Layer Variable, set Data Layer Variable Name to
    ecommerce.value and Data Layer Version to Version 2, name it Ecommerce Value, and save it.

    b. Choose or create the currency variable. For an existing variable, inspect Data Layer Variable
    Name; if creating one, select New > Data Layer Variable, set Data Layer Variable Name to
    ecommerce.currency and Data Layer Version to Version 2, name it Ecommerce Currency, and
    save it.

    c. Choose or create the transaction-ID variable. For an existing variable, inspect Data Layer
    Variable Name; if creating one, select New > Data Layer Variable, set Data Layer Variable Name
    to ecommerce.transaction_id and Data Layer Version to Version 2, name it Ecommerce
    Transaction ID, and save it.

3.4 Create a Custom Event trigger for the GTM4WP purchase event.

    a. In Triggers, select New, choose Custom Event, set Event name to purchase, choose All
    Custom Events, and save the trigger with a clear name such as WooCommerce — Purchase.
         ⚠ Warning: Do not use a Page View trigger for this setup: the purchase event carries the
         order’s dynamic fields and duplicate guard.
         Reference: GTM4WP Google Ads conversion tracking for WooCommerce · Confirm the
         purchase event and ecommerce fields used by the tag.

value, currency, and transaction ID on the purchase event.

    a. Search Tags for enabled Google Ads Conversion Tracking tags that use the same Conversion

    b. If no exact active tag exists, select New > Google Ads Conversion Tracking. Enter the numeric
    Conversion ID and matching Conversion label from WooCommerce purchase tracking note.

    recorded in that note for Conversion Value, Currency Code, and Transaction ID. Set Triggering

    variables.
         ⚠ Warning: A static value or static transaction ID defeats this purchase method.

    c. Save the tag with a clear name such as Google Ads conversion — WooCommerce purchase.
    Reopen it and record its name, IDs, three variables, and trigger under Google Tag Manager in
     WooCommerce purchase tracking note.

         ⚠ Warning: A tag fire proves the browser sent the configured values; it does not prove Google

     b. Reload the same order-received page once in the connected session. In Tag Assistant, confirm

         ⚠ Warning: This browser check does not prove Ads attribution or reporting; transaction-ID
         deduplication is a separate Ads behavior.

4.3 Place a second test order through the same store-provided route with a different quantity,

4.4 If the store uses a status you excluded from tracking, ask the website maintainer for a new

reloading an already-counted order cannot prove this rule works. Record the result under
Preview. If the store does not use any excluded status, record No excluded status used and
continue.

        a. Return to Google Tag Manager, review the workspace changes, and select Submit. Choose

        c. If the connected panel shows Container Version Preview, open Change container

        f. Place a new test order through the route recorded in your note and inspect the purchase event.
     Confirm one conversion tag fire with a non-empty transaction ID, dynamic value, and currency.

           ⚠ Warning: This browser check does not prove Ads attribution or reporting; transaction-ID
```

```text
1. Create the click conversion in Google Ads
Define the exact link click that counts, then save the conversion settings and identifiers you will
use in Tag Manager.

1.1 Choose one link to another page on your website and record its details in a named note.
     ⚠ Warning: This conversion counts the click itself. It does not show that someone completed an
     action on the destination page.

     a. Open a simple note in your notes app or a blank document and name it Click tracking note. At
     the top, add headings Link, Ads settings, Tag Manager, Preview, Live, and Ads status. Under
     Link, leave lines for Full source page URL, Source page path, and Destination URL. You will fill
     the other headings as you work.

     b. In Click tracking note, enter Full source page URL by copying the address of the page
     containing the link. For Destination URL, right-click the chosen link, select Copy link address, and
     paste it into the note. Keep the address exactly as copied, including http:// or https://; it can differ
     from the address shown after the destination page opens.

     c. Find Source page path by copying the part after the website name and before ? or #, if present.
     For example, https://www.example.com/services/?ref=ad has path /services/. Check that the
     destination has the same website name, such as www.example.com, but a different page path.

     d. Open Full source page URL and right-click the chosen link. Choose Copy link address and
     compare it with Destination URL in your note. The link should open an ordinary page on the same
     website, such as a pricing page. A button-shaped link works too if it offers Copy link address.

1.2 Start a manual Google Ads website conversion for the link in Click tracking note.
     ⚠ Warning: Adding a Page view action can create or expose a standard goal. Account-default
     goals can apply to campaigns automatically, so read that state before saving and do not change it in
     this guide.
     Reference: Google Ads: Set up your web conversions · Current website conversion and manual
     setup flow

     a. Open Google Ads, go to Goals > Conversions > Summary, and select + Create conversion
     action. Choose Conversions on a website. In the website URL field, enter Full source page URL
     from Click tracking note: the page where the chosen link appears. Do not enter Source page path
     or Destination URL. Select Scan, continue with the website data source, and keep the Google tag
     data source for this Google Ads and Tag Manager setup.

     b. Open See all if needed and choose Page view as the conversion category. This is the closest
     group for a click leading to another page on your website. Select + Create conversion or + Add
     conversion actions, choose the Google tag data source, and select Manually using code. The
     click trigger you add in Tag Manager will control what counts; loading a page alone will not count.

     c. Before Save and continue, read the current goal list. In Click tracking note, record whether
     Page view is already an account-default goal or whether Google says the new goal will be
     account-default. If the account owner has not decided whether this goal may be used by default,
     resolve that decision before saving. Leave the goal setting unchanged.

1.3 Set the action fields in order before the first save. If the creation screen temporarily forces
Primary, correct that same saved action to Secondary before continuing, then record the final
settings.
     ⚠ Warning: Primary can add this action to the campaign's main conversion totals and help
     automated bid decisions. Secondary keeps it for observation in the standard goal.
     ⚠ Warning: A custom goal is a manually selected bundle of actions; if it includes this Secondary
     action, campaigns using that bundle can still use it for bidding. This guide leaves custom goals
     unchanged.
     Reference: Google Ads: About primary and secondary conversion actions · Action optimization and
     bidding meaning
     Reference: Google Ads: Manage conversion goals from the conversions summary · Account-default
     and custom goal behavior

     a. Name the action Website internal link clicked or another name that identifies the link. Set
     Count to One. One keeps repeated clicks from one ad interaction from becoming several
     conversions.

     b. Choose Secondary when the setting is available. If the creation screen shows Primary and
     does not let you choose Secondary, leave it for this first save and write Initial optimization:
     Primary (temporary) in Click tracking note. A Primary action can influence bidding and the main
     Conversions column, so correct this temporary state on the same action before proceeding. Do not
     use a custom goal as a workaround.

     c. For Value, choose Don't use a value for this conversion action when available. If the screen
     instead asks for a number and currency, enter 0 and select No currency set. This guide counts
     clicks; it does not estimate how much each click is worth.

     d. Under Ads settings in Click tracking note, record the Value option you selected. If you entered
     a number, write 0 — No currency set. You will check the saved setting in the next substep.

     e. Select Done and Save and continue once; do not create another action. Reopen the same
     action. If it was temporarily Primary, change this same action to Secondary, save, and reopen it.
     Confirm Secondary, Count: One, the value meaning, Included in account-level goals: No, the
     goal heading, and the Enhanced conversions state.

     f. If the reopened action shows Enhanced conversions: On, record that state in Click tracking
     note and pause this setup. Ask the account owner or Ads measurement maintainer to review the
     action for a click-only use. Do not change the feature here. Continue only with Off or Not
     configured.

     g. In Click tracking note, record Final optimization, Count, Saved Value setting, Included in
     account-level goals, Goal heading, and Enhanced conversions exactly as shown. For this
     click-only route, Enhanced conversions must read Off or Not configured. If the first save used
     Primary, keep Initial optimization: Primary (temporary) beside the final Secondary setting. Google
     may show a different Value label after saving; record that label here.

Conversion ID and Conversion label into Click tracking note. Keep the pair together. The
Google tag uses AW- plus the numeric ID; the conversion tag uses the numeric ID without AW-.
     ⚠ Warning: A label from another conversion action can send clicks to the wrong action.
     Reference: Google Tag Manager: Google Ads conversions · Where to find and use Conversion ID
     and Conversion label



2. Configure the exact click trigger in Google Tag Manager
Connect the Ads identifiers to the existing Web container while preserving existing coverage
and limiting the conversion to the recorded link.

2.1 Check the Google tag coverage, resolve active ambiguity before adding anything, then
reuse one active site-wide tag or add one when coverage is clear. A tag sends information to
Google; a trigger tells the tag when to send it.
     ⚠ Warning: Overlapping Google tags for the same ID can send duplicate data. Leave paused
     historical tags unchanged.
     Reference: Google Tag Manager: Set up your Google tag · Google tag ID and Initialization trigger

     a. Open Google Tag Manager, select the installed Web container, and open Tags. Search for
     Google tags whose Tag ID is AW- followed by the numeric Conversion ID. In Click tracking note,
     record each matching tag's name, enabled or paused status, and trigger.

     b. Compare the active matches you recorded. If more than one enabled match exists, coverage
     overlaps, or one enabled match uses a trigger other than Initialization - All Pages, do not reuse or
     add a tag. Record each name, Tag ID, status, trigger, and page coverage for the container
     maintainer to choose one route. Leave paused historical tags unchanged.

     c. After the coverage check is clear, reuse the one enabled match that uses Initialization - All
     Pages. If there is no enabled matching tag, leave any paused historical match paused, select New,
     choose Google tag, enter AW- plus the numeric Conversion ID, set Triggering to Initialization - All
     Pages, and save one clear name such as Google tag — Google Ads.

2.2 Check the container's Conversion Linker coverage and record whether the existing Google
tag makes a separate linker unnecessary or whether a linker must be reused or added.
     ⚠ Warning: Two active linkers covering the same page can create conflicting coverage. Resolve
     overlap before adding or enabling another one.
     Reference: Google Tag Manager: Conversion Linker · Linker purpose, All Pages setup, and when a
     separate linker is unnecessary

     a. A Conversion Linker keeps ad-click information in the browser so Google Ads can connect a later
     conversion with the ad interaction. In Tags, inspect every Conversion Linker and the recorded
     Google tag. Record each name, status, trigger, and whether the Google tag loads on every page.
     Treat All Pages as every page and Page Path equals the recorded source path as the chosen
     page.

     b. Before selecting or adding a linker, check the inspected tags for overlap on the relevant pages. If
     active linkers overlap, or a linker trigger is neither All Pages nor Page Path equals the recorded
     source path, pause this setup. Give the person who maintains the container the exact linker names,
     enabled or paused status, triggers, and affected page coverage from Click tracking note, and ask
     them to choose the single coverage path. Leave paused historical linkers paused.

     c. After checking overlap and coverage, write one Linker decision. If the Google tag loads on
     every page, write No separate Conversion Linker needed. Otherwise reuse exactly one active
     linker covering All Pages or the source path; if none does, add one Conversion Linker with All
     Pages and write Added: [tag name]. Never record two decisions.

2.3 Open Variables, select Configure in Built-In Variables, and enable Click URL if it is not
already enabled. Click URL exposes the destination URL from a link-click event so the trigger
can compare it with Click tracking note.
     Reference: Google Tag Manager: Built-in variables for web containers · Click URL behavior

2.4 Create a Just Links trigger that listens only on the source page and fires only for the full
destination URL.
     ⚠ Warning: Do not use All Elements, a broad Click Text match, or a partial destination match;
     those rules can count unrelated clicks.
     Reference: Google Tag Manager: Click trigger · Just Links, filters, Wait for Tags, and Check
     Validation

     a. In Triggers, select New, choose Just Links, and name it Link click — exact target. Just Links
     listens for HTML links; All Elements also listens for buttons, images, and other elements.

     b. Enable Wait for Tags with a 2,000 millisecond timeout and keep Check Validation enabled.
     Under Enable this trigger when all of these conditions are true, set Page Path equals the
     source path in Click tracking note. This listener condition limits the pages where Tag Manager
     watches for links.

     c. Under Some Link Clicks, add two firing filters: Page Path equals the source path and Click
     URL equals the full destination URL in Click tracking note. Both filters must match before the tag
     fires.

     d. Save the trigger and reopen it. Read the listener condition and the two firing filters back into
     Click tracking note.

2.5 Resolve active duplicate or overlap ambiguity before reusing or creating one exact Google
Ads conversion tag.
     ⚠ Warning: Two conversion tags with the same identifiers and overlapping triggers can count the
     same click twice.
     Reference: Google Tag Manager: Google Ads conversions · Conversion tag fields, triggers,
     Preview, and publish

     a. Search active Google Ads Conversion Tracking tags. Compare each candidate’s numeric
     Conversion ID, Conversion label, and trigger with Click tracking note. Reuse one exact match. If
     duplicates, overlap, or paused history make the route unclear, record the names, IDs, labels,
     statuses, and triggers for the container maintainer and do not add another tag.
     b. Only after the duplicate and overlap check is clear, and no exact active match exists, select New,
     choose Google Ads Conversion Tracking, and enter the numeric Conversion ID and matching
     Conversion label from Click tracking note. Leave Conversion Value, Transaction ID, and
     Currency Code empty, keep conversion linking enabled, and select Link click — exact target as
     the trigger.

     c. Save the new tag as Google Ads conversion — Website internal link clicked, then reopen it.
     Record the tag name and its single trigger in Click tracking note.



3. Test and publish the click setup
Check that the conversion stays quiet on page load, fires once for the exact click, and behaves
the same way in the published Live container.

3.1 Check the first page event before the target click.
     ⚠ Warning: Preview shows what the connected browser did in the draft container. Google Ads may
     take time to show the action status, and this check alone does not mean an ad click was counted.
     Reference: Google Tag Manager: Preview and debug containers · Preview and Tag Assistant
     behavior

     a. Select Preview, enter the exact Full source page URL from Click tracking note, and select
     Connect. In Tag Assistant, wait until the first page event appears.

     b. Before clicking anything, open the first page event in Tag Assistant. Confirm the matching Google
     tag fired and follow the one Linker decision in the note: either the one chosen Conversion Linker
     fired or the note says No separate Conversion Linker needed because the Google tag loads on
     every page. Confirm the Google Ads conversion tag is absent from tags that fired for this page
     event.

     c. If the first page event is missing, required Google tag/linker coverage is absent, or the conversion
     tag fires on page load, record the visible result and do not click or publish. Reconnect Preview once
     with Full source page URL. If it is still wrong, give the note to the container maintainer and stop. If it
```

```text
      Manager
1. Confirm the account and recipient
Check the account identity and current user list before opening a new invitation.

1.1 Open Google Ads with your Administrator Google Account. Use the account selector to
choose the account from your prerequisites, then confirm the account name and customer ID
shown in the top bar.

1.2 Open Admin > Access and security > Users. Search for the agency person's exact
Google Account email address. Continue only when the email is not already listed and Google
is not showing a pending invitation for it. If an entry or pending invitation appears, stop and have
the account owner review that existing access instead of sending a duplicate invitation.


2. Send the Standard invitation
Standard is the least-privilege account role for an agency that must manage campaigns and
measurement without managing users or account ownership.

2.1 In Users, click + to add a user. Enter the agency person's exact Google Account email
address. Set Access Expires to the owner-set end date, or leave it at Never only when
ongoing access is authorized. Select Standard under Select account access level. Do not
choose Admin, Billing, Read only, or Email only.

2.2 Click Send invitation only after the recipient, expiration, and Standard role match the
owner's plan. Google sends the invitation email to the address you entered; the agency person
must accept it before access appears as active.
     ⚠ Warning: Sending the invitation contacts the recipient and may give account access after
     acceptance. Check the address before you send.
     Reference: Manage access to your Google Ads account · Review Google's current invitation and
     acceptance behavior.



3. Confirm the saved role and account
A sent email is not proof of access. Check the saved row after the recipient accepts the
invitation.

3.1 After the agency person accepts the invitation, return to Admin > Access and security >
Users and search for the exact email address. Confirm that the saved Access level is
Standard and that Access expires matches the owner's plan. If the row is missing, still
pending, or has another role, stop and have the account owner review it.

3.2 Ask the agency person to sign in with the invited Google Account, open Google Ads, and
confirm that the top bar shows the same customer ID from your prerequisites. Have them
confirm that campaign and report views are available. They should not create or change a
campaign during this check.
```
