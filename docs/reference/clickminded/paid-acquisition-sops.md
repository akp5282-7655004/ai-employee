```text
SOP 145 · PAID ACQUISITION


Research and organize Google Ads
keywords with Semrush
Use Semrush to choose relevant buyer searches, group them around suitable landing pages,
and prepare a prioritized Google Ads keyword plan.



 ESTIMATED TIME           TOOLS                             RESPONSIBLE
 180 minutes              Semrush, Google Sheets            The person planning which searches a
                                                            business should advertise on before a
                                                            Google Search campaign is built.



Before you start
Requirements
  ●​ An existing Semrush account with Keyword Magic Tool and Keyword Overview access and enough
        report/export allowance for the agreed scope.
  ●​ A business website and a person who can confirm its offers, customers, service area and
     advertising budget.
  ●​ A Google account that can make and edit a Google Sheets copy.


 THIS SOP COVERS                                        THIS DOES NOT COVER
       ●​ Buyer-focused keyword discovery across          ●​ SEO page planning or organic ranking
          the agreed offers and markets                       difficulty analysis
       ●​ Business fit, demand, cost uncertainty          ●​ Creating, enabling or optimizing live
          and seasonal patterns                              campaigns
       ●​ Campaign/ad-group/page mapping, initial         ●​ Guaranteed traffic, conversion, revenue
          match types and proposed negatives                 or profitability forecasts
       ●​ Prioritized owner-review handoff with           ●​ Competitor-brand bidding and legal or
          gaps and next actions                              trademark decisions

Related SOPs
  ●​    SOP 129: Review Search terms and add negative keywords in Google Ads
  ●​    SOP 149: Add sitelinks, callouts and structured snippets to Google Search ads
  ●​    SOP 130: Write and save a responsive Search ad in Google Ads
  ●​    SOP 039: Choose the right Google Ads conversion tracking method


1. Define the business and research coverage
1.1 Make your own copy of the Search Campaign Planning Workbook. Rename it for the
business and research date. Read Guide, then Example; fill Brief and Keywords with your own
facts.

1.2 Ask the person responsible for advertising which offers to promote and what a visitor should
do after clicking. In Brief, fill Owner, Business / offer, Customer action, Locations, Target country,
Target language, Landing pages and Excluded offers/audiences. Open each listed page to
confirm what it actually sells. Enter the site URL in Brief → Website and set Brief → Network to
Google.

     a. Treat a landing page as the page the ad will open. It must explain the offer and let the visitor take
     the desired action. For example, a furniture-restoration enquiry belongs on the restoration-service
     page, not a shop selling new furniture.

     b. If an intended offer has no suitable page, write “Page needed” beside that offer in Brief →
     Landing pages. Keep researching it, but later mark its keywords Hold until a named person supplies
     a usable page.

1.3 Ask the owner for the planned Monthly budget and Research currency, Primary conversion
and Target CPA/ROAS; enter them in Brief. A conversion is the useful action being counted,
such as a completed enquiry. Cost per acquisition (CPA) is the allowed advertising cost for one
conversion. Return on ad spend (ROAS) compares conversion value with ad cost. Enter “Not
provided” for missing figures; research can continue, but affordability will need owner review.

1.4 In Brief → Seed coverage, list the offers and the different ways buyers could describe each
one. A seed is a starting phrase for discovery. Include the service/product name, purchase or
booking words, and relevant location or product details. For restoration, try “furniture
restoration,” “chair restoration service” and “furniture repair Bristol.” Record brand searches
separately if the owner wants them.


2. Discover and retain keyword ideas in Semrush
2.1 Open Semrush Keyword Magic Tool. Select Brief → Target country as the database, enter
one seed and run the search. Set the Language filter to Brief → Target language. Enter today’s
date in Brief → Research date and the displayed currency in Research currency. In Keywords,
enter the country/language/Google under Market/language/network. Under Data period, record
the displayed metric date; if none is shown, write “Current database snapshot; checked [today’s
date].”
        ⚠ Warning: If the account cannot show or export the needed results, ask the owner for access or a
        permitted export. Do not treat a limited report as complete.

2.2 Start with Broad Match to explore the seed’s wording. Review the side-panel groups and
Related results to find different relevant buyer descriptions. Use Include/Exclude keywords to
narrow to the offers in Brief; use Intent as a helpful starting filter, then read each phrase
yourself. Research each useful new theme and each remaining seed.

        a. Broad Match, Phrase Match and Exact Match here filter Semrush’s suggested phrases. They do
        not set how a Google ad will match searches. Later you will choose Google Ads match types
        separately in the workbook.

2.3 Use Export → CSV for each chosen result scope and retain the original files in your normal
project folder with the workbook. Map reviewed candidates into Keywords: Research batch,
Tool, Keyword, Monthly searches, Seasonality, Estimated CPC and Paid competition. Keep the
displayed currency with CPC and mark unavailable data “Not available.”

        a. Volume is estimated monthly demand; Trend shows its pattern. CPC estimates the cost of an ad
        click; Com. or Competitive Density describes paid advertiser competition. KD% describes difficulty
        ranking in unpaid results, so do not use it to reject a paid keyword. None of these metrics proves a
        keyword will convert.

        b. Check the chosen scope’s row count against the export. When an export limit truncates it, split
        the scope into non-overlapping themes or filters and export every part. Record those parts and any
        unfinished scope in Brief → Seed coverage and Research completeness. A few selected rows or the
        first page is not the full research.

2.4 For a local business, inspect each promising group’s main phrase in Keyword Overview
with the target city or region when available. Read which metrics are local and which remain
national. Add the local scope, data date and observed figures to the matching Keywords row’s
Reason/next action. If local data is unavailable, keep the national figures labeled national and
mark local demand uncertain; a city name in a phrase is not proof the search came from that
city.


3. Screen phrases and map the campaign structure
3.1 Review every collected phrase in Keywords. Fill Search intent with what the person
probably wants and Offer fit with Yes, Unclear or No. Read the whole phrase: “furniture repair
service” suggests hiring help; “furniture repair course” suggests training. Mark clearly unrelated
phrases Exclude in Decision and explain why in Reason/next action. Put uncertain phrases on
Hold and write the exact question for the owner.

3.2 For each remaining distinct buyer task, open its proposed page and compare the promise
with the phrase. Fill Landing page in Keywords with the actual URL. A page offering chair
restoration can serve “chair restoration service”; a page about antique valuations cannot answer
that request. Keep a useful phrase on Hold when its page is missing, misleading or cannot
complete the desired action. In Reason/next action, name the page change and who should
supply it.

3.3 Combine repeated instances of the same phrase from the same market into one working
row. Keep the Research batch references in Reason/next action so you can trace its data. Keep
separate rows when market or language differs. Group close variations that mean the same task
together; do not add their search volumes together as a count of different people.

3.4 Fill Campaign and Ad group for each phrase you may build. A campaign holds a shared
budget and settings; an ad group holds keywords that can use the same ad message and
landing page. Start with one campaign for an offer/market that can share those settings. Split
when the owner needs a separate budget, location or language.

3.5 Within each campaign, give the same Ad group name to phrases with the same buyer task,
offer and page. “Chair restoration service” and “restore antique chair” can share a
chair-restoration group. “Sofa upholstery” needs another group and a suitable upholstery page.
Put overlapping synonyms in the same group rather than creating competing copies of the same
theme.

3.6 Fill Initial match type for each build candidate. For this first plan, recommend Exact for a
specific buyer task; it can also match searches with the same meaning or intent. Use Phrase
when the offer and page can answer meaningful additions to the phrase and the owner can
review search terms after launch. Phrase can cover more searches than Exact. Do not duplicate
every phrase in both types.

     a. For an antique-chair specialist, recommend Exact for “antique chair restoration.” Recommend
     Phrase for “chair restoration service” only if different chair types really are served. Add the reason in
     Reason/next action. These are Google Ads match types, not literal guarantees about the words
     people must type.

     b. Keep Broad for a later, separately reviewed expansion when conversion measurement and
     Smart Bidding are ready. Smart Bidding automatically adjusts bids toward a conversion goal. Broad
     can reach related searches beyond the phrase’s direct meaning, so it needs a deliberate
     measurement and bidding plan.

3.7 Use excluded phrases to propose negative keywords in Keywords → Negative keyword,
Negative match and Negative scope. A negative keyword blocks searches you do not want. For
a service business that does not teach, propose Phrase negative “restoration course” at that
campaign’s scope; it blocks searches containing those words in that order, including extra
words. Use Exact negative for only one unwanted full search.

     a. Read every proposed negative against the build candidates before accepting it. Do not block
     “repair” when furniture repair is the service you sell. Avoid account-wide scope for an exclusion that
     another offer needs. In Reason/next action, explain the unwanted request and why the chosen
     scope is safe.

     b. List needed singular/plural forms and synonyms separately: negative keywords do not expand
     like positive keywords. For example, excluding “course” does not automatically exclude “courses” or
     “training.” Keep the proposed list in this workbook for campaign review.



4. Compare costs, prioritize and hand off the plan
4.1 Compare Estimated CPC only for rows with the same market, date basis and currency. Use
it as an estimate of relative click cost. In Brief → Assumed conversion rate/source, record an
owner-supplied or clearly labeled planning assumption if available. For lead generation, a rough
affordable CPC equals Target CPA × assumed conversion rate. Record the calculation in
Reason/next action; it is a planning check, not a bid instruction or forecast.

     a. Fictional example: an owner accepts £50 per enquiry and assumes 5% of clicks become
     enquiries. £50 × 0.05 = £2.50 per click. A £5 estimated CPC needs owner review before it ranks
     ahead of an equally relevant £2 candidate. A missing target or conversion rate means “Affordability
     not assessed” in Reason/next action, not a made-up pass or automatic exclusion. For a ROAS goal,
     ask the owner for an allowable click cost using expected conversion value and rate.

     b. If you also use Keyword Planner data, retain separate source/date/market references in
     Reason/next action. Do not average Semrush CPC with Google’s top-of-page bid range: they
     measure different things. Investigate scope or date differences before changing the business
     decision.

4.2 Compare the groups, not just isolated phrases. Put Build first on the clearest buyer tasks
with a suitable page, owner priority, relevant demand and a plausible cost to investigate. Put
Build next on relevant, page-ready groups that are weaker on those comparisons or would
stretch the available budget. Use Hold for missing facts/pages or unresolved cost concerns; use
Exclude for the wrong offer or audience. Write a reason and a numbered Priority order for the
build groups.

      a. Fictional example: “chair restoration service” with 90 searches and a suitable enquiry page can
      rank ahead of “furniture ideas” with 9,000 searches because the latter does not ask to hire the
      service. A relevant “sofa upholstery” group waits on Hold when there is no upholstery page. If two
      groups both fit, prefer the owner’s more important offer, then clearer buying intent and a ready page;
      use demand and cost to break the remaining tie.

      b. When demand or cost estimates are missing, keep “Not available” in the metric cell and explain
      the missing check in Reason/next action. Do not replace a missing value with zero or drop a precise
      buyer phrase solely for low volume. If you cannot justify an order, put the affected group on Hold and
      name the person or evidence needed.

4.3 Return to Brief → Seed coverage. For every included offer, check that you researched its
main buyer descriptions, reviewed useful newly discovered themes, and assessed every
collected candidate. Repeat discovery for uncovered themes. Finish when another relevant
expansion produces only repeats or out-of-scope phrases, or record the exact unfinished area
and access/quota limit in Brief → Research completeness. Add more rows as needed; template
rows and export limits do not define completion.

4.4 Reopen your saved workbook. Filter Keywords to Build first and Build next. Check the
market/date context, Decision, Priority order, Campaign, Ad group, Landing page, Initial match
type and Reason/next action for every included phrase. Open the landing pages again, then
read proposed negatives against the included phrases. Fix blanks, contradictory groups and
unsupported claims. In Brief → Plan status, write “Ready for owner review” or “Incomplete” with
the remaining question and owner.

4.5 Send the workbook to the person responsible for campaign setup using your normal
handoff process. Ask them to review the cost assumptions, match types, negatives and
unresolved questions before building.
```

```text
SOP 141 · PAID ACQUISITION


Diagnose and fix common Merchant Center
product disapprovals
Find what is blocking your products on Google and correct the source of the problem.



 ESTIMATED TIME            TOOLS                             RESPONSIBLE
 75 minutes                Google Merchant Center,           The person who maintains the store’s
                           Woocommerce, Google For           product catalog and needs to get blocked
                           Woocommerce, Google               Google listings working again.
                           Sheets



Before you start
Requirements
  ●​ Merchant Center access to view product/setup issues and request reviews when offered.
  ●​ Access to edit the product source sending data to Google; for this procedure, a WooCommerce
        catalog connected through Google for WooCommerce.
  ●​ The store owner can confirm actual stock, prices, identifiers, selling priorities, and business/policy
        information; the store maintainer can repair website or sync problems.


 THIS SOP COVERS                                        THIS DOES NOT COVER
       ●​ Account-level versus product-level               ●​ Guaranteeing Google approval or
           diagnosis                                           bypassing review delays
       ●​ Common product-data, page, and image             ●​ Completing identity verification or
           corrections at the WooCommerce source               legal/policy appeals for the owner
       ●​ Prioritizing corrections across the              ●​ Changing campaign budgets, billing, or
          affected catalog                                    launch
       ●​ Rechecking current country/destination           ●​ Other ecommerce platforms or
          status and using Google’s offered review            manual-feed setup
          route

Related SOPs
  ●​    SOP 138: Set up Merchant Center and verify the store website
  ●​    SOP 139: Configure Merchant Center shipping and returns
  ●​    SOP 140: Connect WooCommerce products to Merchant Center
  ●​    SOP 142: Link Merchant Center to an owned Google Ads account
  ●​    SOP 143: Build and review a first Standard Shopping campaign before launch
1. Find the issues and decide what to repair first
1.1 Open the Product issue log worksheet and make a copy. Read Guide, then review the
fictional cases in Example. Enter your own findings in Issues, starting at row 2.

1.2 Open Merchant Center and select the store’s account. Confirm its website. Ask the store
owner which selling countries and products are in scope and which products matter most to
sales. Create a short note called Review scope and write those answers there.

1.3 Open Products > Needs attention, then View setup and policy issues. Read any banner,
warning deadline, or suspension. Enter each issue in Issues with the account ID under Product
ID or account, the exact issue/country/destination, and Level = Account.

1.4 In Products > Needs attention, read the product issue groups. Open each relevant group
and its affected products. Enter the exact Google product ID, issue text, country and destination,
affected count, and Level = Product in Issues. Use Google’s download control for a large list,
then work through it in batches.

1.5 In Priority and reason, put account-wide blocks and warning deadlines first. Next, choose
shared errors that affect many products the store can currently sell. Use the owner’s selling
priorities to break ties. Put missing-input cases behind fixes you can complete, while asking the
owner for those inputs.

1.6 Open a blocked product and inspect its data source and current values. Match its ID and
link to the WooCommerce product or variation. In Source and correction, name the field or
shared rule supplying the wrong value, and in Owner name who can change it.


2. Correct the common source problems
2.1 For missing website verification/claim or account setup, complete the linked procedure and
reread View setup and policy issues. For a policy suspension, give the owner Google’s exact
issue and required actions; correct product data while the owner resolves the account-level
requirements.
     ⚠ Warning: Use accurate business information. Creating another Merchant Center account does
     not resolve the original policy problem.
     Then follow: SOP 138: Set up Merchant Center and verify the store website

2.2 For missing or conflicting shipping/returns, compare Merchant Center with the store’s
published policy and checkout. Use the linked procedure to correct the actual policy settings,
then reread the issue in Merchant Center.
     Then follow: SOP 139: Configure Merchant Center shipping and returns

2.3 For a price error, open the exact WooCommerce product or variation. Check regular price,
sale price/dates, currency, and tax display against the public product page and cart. Correct the
product field or sale schedule that is wrong and click Update. Give a store-wide currency/tax
problem to the maintainer.

2.4 For an availability error, compare WooCommerce Product data > Inventory with actual stock
and the public product’s buying controls. Correct the stock status/quantity using the owner’s
stock facts and click Update. Ask the maintainer to fix a broken buying button when stock is
correct.

2.5 For an identifier error, check the exact product or variation’s packaging or manufacturer
record. In Product data → Inventory, correct GTIN, UPC, EAN, or ISBN. For a variable
product, use each variation’s native barcode field. In Product data → Google for
WooCommerce, correct Brand and MPN where applicable. GTIN is the manufacturer’s
barcode; MPN is its part number. Save the variation changes, then Update the product.

2.6 For a condition error, set the product’s Google Condition field to match what is sold and
click Update. Use Used for an opened or second-hand item. Use Refurbished only for a
professionally restored item with a warranty. Make the public description agree.

2.7 For missing title or description, edit the WooCommerce product name and description to
explain the specific item the buyer receives. For missing category attributes, open Marketing >
Google for WooCommerce > Attributes and correct the relevant saved mapping rule.

2.8 For an unavailable landing page, open Google’s submitted link while signed out on desktop
and mobile. Check that it opens the advertised item/variation without a login and can reach the
cart. Correct the product link at the source if wrong; give access blocks or broken pages to the
maintainer.

2.9 For an image error, open the submitted image URL. Replace a missing, wrong, or
promotional image with a clear image of the actual product or variation, preferably at least 500 ×
500 pixels, then Update. Ask the maintainer to remove an unintended crawler block when the
image itself is correct.

2.10 After correcting a shared rule or field, inspect another affected product to confirm it has the
intended value. Apply the same correction to the remaining affected products with that cause.
Keep exceptions separate rather than overwriting every product with one value.

2.11 If the saved correction does not reach Google, open Marketing → Google for
WooCommerce → Product Feed and read the sync error. In Source and correction, record
the product ID, changed field, save time and error. Name the store maintainer under Owner and
ask them to investigate using WooCommerce’s troubleshooting guidance. Have them plan any
disconnect/reconnect for this existing connection before making that change. Record their repair
and next check time in Next action or check date, then repeat the received-value check after
sync resumes.

2.12 For every repaired row, write the saved change in Source and correction. Enter the person
responsible in Owner and the latest Google status in Current Google status. For unresolved
cases, give a concrete next action instead of writing Fixed.


3. Check Google’s result and follow up
3.1 After the source syncs, reopen the same product in Merchant Center. Compare the
corrected value with WooCommerce and the live page. Enter the check date and time, including
time zone, in Checked at.

3.2 Open the affected issue’s details. If Google offers Request website check or Request
review after you have verified the repair, read its requirements and use that action. For an
account issue, ensure all listed problems are resolved before choosing I fixed the issue. Let the
owner handle identity documents or a disagreement appeal.

3.3 Reopen the same products and account issues after processing or review. Compare their
status for the same country and destination. Enter the current status in Current Google status
and the date/time in Checked at. Record the next check date for Pending or Under review.

3.4 Continue through every issue group in the chosen catalog. Add worksheet rows as needed;
the prepared rows are not a task limit. Check that each row has an owner and either a cleared
result or a specific remaining action and check date.

3.5 Give the completed Product issue log to the store owner and maintainer. Point out account
blocks first, then remaining product repairs, review waits, and missing inputs. Keep excluded
products identified as Excluded rather than calling them approved.
```

## SOP 139: Configure Merchant Center shipping and returns

<details><summary>Full SOP text</summary>

```text
SOP 139 · PAID ACQUISITION


Configure Merchant Center shipping and
returns
Match Google’s shipping costs, delivery estimates, and return information to the policies
shoppers see on your store.



  ESTIMATED TIME             TOOLS                            RESPONSIBLE
  50 minutes                 Google Merchant Center           People responsible for making a store’s
                                                              product listings show accurate shipping
                                                              and return information.



Before you start
Requirements
  ●​ Access that allows you to edit the store’s Merchant Center shipping and return settings
  ●​ Merchant Center connected to the correct store website
  ●​ The intended Merchant Center account name and ID, plus the store URL, from the setup handoff
        or store owner
  ●​ The store’s current public shipping and return pages, checkout, and access to the owner or
        fulfillment team for missing facts
  ●​ Countries where the store sells and can fulfill orders, with standard shipping rules that apply
        uniformly to products within each country


  THIS SOP COVERS                                         THIS DOES NOT COVER
       ●​ Manual account-level online shipping              ●​ Carrier-calculated, weight-based,
            policies for every intended selling country        product-specific, and postcode-specific
       ●​   One standard delivery service per                  delivery rules
            country with uniform product coverage           ●​ Product labels for shipping or return
       ●​   Free, flat-rate, or order-value shipping           exceptions
            with an optional free-shipping threshold        ●​ Drafting legal store policies or changing
       ●​   Manual handling and transit times                  fulfillment promises
       ●​   A standard return policy per country and        ●​ Product uploads, tax setup, express
            saved-policy readback                              delivery programs, and campaign launch

Related SOPs
  ●​    SOP 138: Set up Merchant Center and verify the store website
  ●​    SOP 140: Connect WooCommerce products to Merchant Center
  ●​    SOP 141: Diagnose and fix common Merchant Center product disapprovals
  ●​    SOP 142: Link Merchant Center to an owned Google Ads account
  ●​ SOP 143: Build and review a first Standard Shopping campaign before launch


1. Turn store policies into settings
1.1 Open the store’s shipping and returns pages. Create a temporary note called Store policy
notes. At the top, add an Account section with Account name, Account ID, and Store URL;
enter those values from the setup handoff or owner. Below it, create one section per selling
country using the following policy fields.

     a. Add these fields: Country; product-price currency; covered products; shipping charge and any
     free-shipping threshold; minimum order value, if any; order cutoff and timezone; handling-day range
     and working days; transit-day range and delivery days.

     b. Add these fields: Return-policy URL; returns accepted for defective and non-defective goods;
     exchanges; accepted product condition; return window; return methods; label provider; return
     shipping payer and charge; restocking fee; refund processing days; seasonal window extensions.

1.2 For each country, compare the public shipping page with an ordinary product’s checkout
quote before payment. Enter the store’s actual shipping charges and covered products in Store
policy notes. Ask the owner to resolve conflicts before saving Merchant Center settings.
     ⚠ Warning: An account-wide rule may affect existing listings. If a connector or store platform
     manages these settings, agree which system owns them first; a later sync can overwrite manual
     edits.

1.3 Ask the fulfillment team when orders start processing, when the carrier collects them, and
how long delivery normally takes. Enter those facts in the delivery fields of Store policy notes.

     a. Use handling for processing before carrier pickup and transit for travel after pickup. Example
     only: packing takes 1–2 working days and carrier delivery takes 3–5 working days. Enter 1–2 for
     handling and 3–5 for transit; do not enter the full 4–7 days twice.

     b. Enter the order cutoff, its timezone, processing weekdays, and carrier delivery weekdays. A
     Friday order after cutoff may start on Monday when processing runs Monday–Friday. Ask the
     fulfillment team for missing details; a guessed same-day promise is not usable.

1.4 Fill the return fields in Store policy notes from the public return page. Ask the owner for
any missing terms before configuring that country. Separate a change-of-mind return from a
defective item; the store may treat them differently.
     ⚠ Warning: A restocking fee is a deduction for accepting the returned item. Return postage pays to
     send it back. Choosing “No cost” for restocking does not make return postage free.



2. Configure shipping for each country
2.1 Open Merchant Center. Confirm the account using the Account section of Store policy
notes, then open the standard shipping policy for the first country.

     a. Choose the account matching Account name and Account ID from Merchant Center’s available
     account choices. Check the selected account ID against Store policy notes. If the ID differs or
     several accounts remain unresolved, stop and ask the owner to identify the intended account.

     b. Open Settings → Business info and compare the saved online store URL with Store URL in
     Store policy notes. If it differs, stop and resolve the account with the owner before changing
     settings.

     c. Select Products & store → Shipping and returns → Shipping policies. For the first country in
     Store policy notes, edit its existing standard policy. When none exists, select Add shipping
     policy, or Get Started on a new account. If asked to add selling countries first, select the intended
     countries from the note and continue to the shipping policy.

2.2 Name the policy so your team can recognize its country and service, such as “UK standard
delivery”. Select the country from Store policy notes and All products for its uniform standard
service. Use one policy per country in this procedure.

2.3 In Delivery times, enter the delivery values from Store policy notes in the cutoff, handling,
and transit fields. If the screen first offers a choice of delivery-time methods, choose the
manual-entry option.

     a. Set the cutoff time and timezone. Enter the minimum and maximum handling days and select the
     weekdays orders are processed. Use 0 handling days only when the store really ships orders the
     same day before cutoff.

     b. Set the transit range for all destinations in this country and the carrier’s delivery weekdays shown
     by the form. Use the transit values, not the handling-plus-transit total.

2.4 Choose the product-price currency from Store policy notes, then use the cost option
matching the checkout charge.

     a. For genuinely free shipping on every order, select free shipping. For one fee on every order,
     select flat rate and enter that fee. For charges that change by order total, use the order-value rate
     table and enter every price band from the store’s policy.
     b. Set a free-shipping threshold or a minimum order value only when the store has that condition.
     Example only: shipping is £4 below £50 and free from £50. Enter the paid rate and the £50
     threshold; do not select unconditional free shipping.

2.5 Review and save the policy. Reopen it from Shipping policies. Read back country,
covered products, currency, charges and order conditions, cutoff and timezone, handling days
and weekdays, and transit days and weekdays against Store policy notes. Repeat this
milestone for every intended selling country.


3. Configure the return policy
3.1 In Shipping and returns → Return policies, edit the existing standard policy for the
country, or select Add return policy when none exists. Enter the public Return-policy URL
from Store policy notes and choose the countries whose terms are identical.

3.2 Choose returns and exchanges to match Store policy notes. Accepting defective and
non-defective products means shoppers can also return an unwanted item; defective-only is a
different promise.

3.3 Select the allowed product condition and return window from Store policy notes. Add a
seasonal window extension only if the public policy has one.

3.4 Set the methods and fees from Store policy notes.

     a. Select the supported return methods. For mail returns, choose how shoppers receive the label:
     download/print, in the box, or customer responsibility. Match who provides the label to the public
     policy.

     b. Select the policy currency, enter the restocking fee, and enter refund processing days. Complete
     any return-postage payer or charge field shown by the form using the note. Keep label provision,
     return postage, and restocking as separate choices.
          ⚠ Warning: If the form cannot describe a material return cost, keep the public policy explicit
          and resolve the unsupported setting with the owner before treating it as a matching setup.

3.5 Review the summary against Store policy notes. Select I confirm the information I
entered matches the return policy on my website only when it matches the public policy,
then save. Reopen the saved return policy and compare its URL, countries, returns and
exchanges, condition, window and extensions, methods, label provider, postage terms shown,
currency, restocking fee, and refund processing days with the note. Repeat for countries with
different standard terms.


4. Check what shoppers will be promised
4.1 For each configured country, open the store checkout before payment with an ordinary
product. Check a normal order and, where relevant, orders just below, exactly at, and above the
free-shipping threshold. Compare the shipping quote and delivery promise with the reopened
Merchant Center policy. Add results under Checkout comparison in that country’s Store
policy notes section.

4.2 In Store policy notes, add Policy status for each return policy and any setting still
awaiting correction or Google review. Give the note to the owner and person managing the
product catalog. Ask that person to check whether product-level shipping values or a connector
replace these account settings.
```
